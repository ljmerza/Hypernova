# Hypernova design system

The base comes from 0stems (atmosphere, colour, type), the structure from
Hyperscribe (the seam, pills, quiet copy), and urgent states from
Hypernavigate (the filled block). On Android it's implemented in
`android/app/src/main/java/com/prudnikweb/hypernova/ui/`. The home screen
widgets repeat them as Android resources. (The Mac app's SwiftUI port, "Mac menu bar and mini app",
and Lens, "The Lens guide" and "Lens (G2)", are no longer in this repository; those sections stay
as history.)

## Type
- **Manrope** (variable, weight 500 almost everywhere, `ss01` and `tnum`). The wordmark uses weight 200.
- **JetBrains Mono** for terminals, hex ids, paths and numbers in meters.
- Labels: 10sp uppercase, tracking 0.18em, weight 600.
- Sizes: display 34, title 22, heading 16, body 15, small 13, mono 12/11.

## Atmosphere
- **One clock** (`fx/Ambient.kt`): the cosmos, grain, the planet meter, pulsing dots and the terminal cursor all read a single `AmbientClock`. It runs at 30fps (20 on battery saver), sleeps between frames instead of waking on every vsync, stops when the app isn't visible, and freezes under Reduce Motion. Animations read it only in draw/layer lambdas, so ticks never recompose. There are no infinite transitions anywhere.
- **Cosmos** (`fx/Cosmos.kt`): a port of `DigitalCosmos.tsx`.
  - Kept from the original: 6 clusters, 4 nebulae (and their breathing), wind 0.04 rad/s. Not its 2-character hex labels beside the stars: in an app whose sessions have hex ids, the owner read them as "faint stray ids" over the sky (2026-10-18). The random draws that chose them are still made, so the stars stay where they were. The stars are new (owner's note, 2026-10-16: they "look like dots", unlike the sun).
  - **Stars like real stars** (`fx/Stars.kt`, pixels in `fx/SkyArt.kt`): 200 of them, in the sun's own family: a core white-hot at its centre and coloured outward, a soft halo, and for the brighter ones a wide faint bloom and thin diffraction spikes (a long horizontal pair, the vertical one 0.62 as long, as the sun's).
    - Colours follow temperature in a real sky's proportions: a few blue-white, more white and yellow-white, most of them orange and red; the fainter the star, the redder it's likely to be.
    - Brightness follows a real sky too, about three times as many stars in each fainter tier: 3.5% bright (spikes 17dp), 11% medium (spikes 8dp), 28.5% faint (a soft point with a small glow), 57% very faint.
    - No twinkle and no breathing: there's no air in space. The sun doesn't twinkle either.
    - The 24 sprites (4 tiers × 6 colours) are baked once per density into one atlas, on a background thread. A frame draws one bitmap per star, and the GPU batches them. The field comes from the sky's seed, so it's the same sky at every launch; at launch the stars fade in over 0.7s while the atlas is made.
  - **Deep-sky objects** (`fx/DeepSky.kt`, pixels in `fx/SkyArt.kt`), as Hubble photographs them, few and faint, never competing with the sessions:
    - A face-on spiral: blue arms of knots and feathery spurs, dust lanes on their inner edges, pink star-forming nebulae, a yellow-white core, sometimes a bar.
    - An edge-on galaxy cut by its dark dust lane, and a soft elliptical with a few globular clusters round it.
    - Star-forming pillars like the Pillars of Creation, in Hubble's teal and gold: rust dust lit gold and white on the edges facing the young stars above, heads boiling off into haze.
    - A ring nebula like M57: blue-green inside, a ring from yellow-green to red with dark knots and spokes, faint red loops beyond.
    - A globular cluster: 1,500 old stars crowding into a glowing core.
    - A corner of deep field against one screen edge: tiny spirals, red ellipticals, blue clumpy galaxies and a foreground star with spikes.
    - Placement: two of the three big ones (spiral, edge-on, pillars) share the open sky below the solar system, one on each side, scaled to fit it. The rest lie lower, behind the list, showing between its cards. The deep field sits a little beyond its edge, so the tilt never shows its cut. Where each one goes, and its shape and turn, come from the sky's seed: different for every install, the same at every launch.
    - Each picture is light added up per pixel and rolled off softly at the top (no clipped cores), fading to nothing before its edge. They're rendered off the main thread into bitmaps (about 1.7MB at 3× density), each fading in over 2.5s as it arrives.
    - They sit in the farthest layer and don't drift. Behind text they step back to 30%. They thin with the week like the galaxy; the week's limit leaves them as they are.
  - Nebula radius is minSide × 0.30–0.55 × 1.5. 0stems applies dpr twice, so on a phone its clouds come out at this size.
  - **Not on Home any more** (owner, 2026-10-30): Home is the sessions in their groups, the list starting under its top bar, with only the starfield behind it; the mark in the top bar opens Statistics, as the sun did, and the planets' project filter went with the sky. The solar system below stays hidden in the code (`skyVisibility` 0 on Home) and here as history.
  - **One solar system, and the sun is your usage** (owner's idea, 2026-10-13; `fx/Solar.kt`). Usage is per account, so it's one sun rather than one per project.
    - **The sun is an accretion disk, and the disk is the 5-hour window** (owner, 2026-10-18: the supernova, the black hole and the reborn star weren't physics: "this should be truly from physics point of view, but still, which would reflect the limits and so on, usage for instance"; DECISIONS, "The sky, physically true"; `fx/Disk.kt`, `fx/SkyPhysics.kt`). The sun is a **cataclysmic variable** as a dwarf nova is: a **white dwarf** at the centre, the **accretion disk** round it, and the **donor**, a small cool star on a tilted orbit just outside the rim that feeds the disk through a **stream**. Seen from 20° above the disk's plane like the rest of the system. The white dwarf survives everything; nothing is born and nothing dies. What the window does, the disk does, each with a real name:
      - **The hot disk is the budget left.** A fresh window is a full disk in outburst: white-gold at its inner edge (2.6dp), through gold and orange to its rim (11.5dp), as a thin disk is hottest inside (T ∝ r^-¾). As you spend, the **cooling front** moves in from the rim, as a dwarf nova's disk cools from its rim in: the hot part reaches out to where the budget left puts it, by area, not width (`DiskModel.hotRadius`: a disk's mass grows with its area, so the last tenth is a thin inner ring, not a tenth of the width). The spent part outside the front keeps its gas, **cool and deep red**, thin at its edge. The light falls as the window is spent: the disk's warm haze in the space round it, and the light on the planets (60% at a quiet disk, never dark), go with the hot area.
      - **The spend rate is the light.** The light of a disk is its accretion rate (L = GMṀ/R). While something works and spends, **knots** of hotter gas spiral in through the hot disk, orbiting at Kepler speeds (ω ∝ r^-1.5; the inner edge goes round in 9 s, since a white dwarf's inner disk really does take seconds) and drifting inward at the flow's rate, and the **inner edge glows** with it. The flow is the pace as the meters have it: 1 at the even pace (the window spent exactly by its reset), up to 2.5 when spending much faster, 0 at rest (`DiskModel.flow`). At rest nothing flows and nothing moves: a smooth, still disk, and the sky asks for the far sky's slow frames only. Knots and glow are the only motion the window has; there is no pulse, no breath.
      - **The projected limit** stays the ring's: the pace arc and the red notch. Nothing in the physics forecasts; the gauge does.
      - **The limit:** the cooling front reaches the inner edge. Watched, the last hot gas drains onto the white dwarf over a moment or two (the usage eases to 100%) with a last flush at the inner edge; found later, the disk is simply quiet. **Quiescence:** a cool, dim, deep-red disk, the white dwarf a blue-white point at its centre, and the **bright spot** where the stream lands on the rim now the brightest warm thing in the system (as it is in a real quiescent dwarf nova). Names, orbits and the needs-you brackets step back to 70%, as they did round the hole. No flash, no overlay, no full-screen white: running out of budget is a quiet thing. (The heavy haptic at the limit stays: it marks the limit, not an explosion.)
      - **The wait: the refill.** Through the wait the stream from the donor refills the quiet disk: its density grows with the countdown's progress (in 1/40 steps, so a disk that waits for hours isn't a new gradient every slow frame), its stream and bright spot stay lit. The readout under it keeps counting: `REFILLS IN 0:24`, rounded up to the second and redrawn when its text changes, as every countdown is (`fx/Countdown.kt`), so it reads what the account card under it reads. The ring is not drawn while the disk is quiet (the line says it all); nothing moves, so the sky asks for slow frames only.
      - **The reset: the outburst.** The **heating front** sweeps the refilled disk from its rim inward over 1.6 s (where the stream piled the gas: a dwarf nova's outside-in outburst), the disk flares back to a full hot disk with a flush of extra light that settles over 1.5 s, names come back to full, and the new window's number fades in. A reset that passed more than 10 s before the app saw it is simply a full disk again: no outburst, no event.
      - **The donor is the window's clock.** It goes once round per window (a cataclysmic variable's orbit is hours long; this one's is the window), so its place on its orbit is the window's time, like the ring's time arc: the top is the reset, clockwise (`DiskModel.donorAngle`); with no reset known it stands at the top. It is a dull red star at 17dp, drawn out toward the white dwarf into its Roche point and lit on the side that faces the disk. Its stream curves forward from the point to the rim and lands ahead of the donor (`STREAM_LEAD`, 0.9 rad: gas leaving the donor keeps its orbital motion), faint while the disk is in outburst, bright in quiescence. It moves a pixel every few minutes, so it costs no frames.
      - **Nothing working:** no flow. The knots are gone, the inner edge's glow is at its floor, the disk holds its size, and the sky is still (a Z Cam standstill, if a name is wanted: a disk that neither drains nor refills).
      - **Mac asleep, paused, numbers stale:** nothing is measured, so nothing is claimed: the disk holds as last seen, the ring holds its numbers, no flow. (Signed out, no plan, on hold: the eclipse, below.)
      - **The weekly limit** in the system: the donor's mass transfer stops, a **VY Sculptoris low state**: the stream goes out and the disk drains to nothing, the white dwarf a faint point with the donor still on its orbit. Nothing else ends (see "The week" below).
      - **Per-model weeks (Fable):** not a body in the sky. A model's week is a slice of the same budget, not a second reservoir; it stays on the meters and the usage card.
      - **Kepler everywhere something goes round** (`fx/SkyPhysics.kt`, `Kepler`): the disk's knots (ω ∝ r^-1.5), the moons' shells (the innermost in 6 s, each outer shell slower by (r/r₀)^1.5: 6, about 10, about 14.5 s), the donor (one window). The planets stand still on their fixed places: at their distances a year is days, so nothing of their orbits shows in five hours; what moves on human timescales is a planet's spin and its moons, which is what moves here.
      - **Sizes and layout:** the disk's rim is the sun's size everywhere the layout reads it (the ring at max(20dp, 1.55 × 11.5 + 6 = 23.8dp), the readout under it, the hit area out to the donor's orbit), and it never changes, so nothing in the band moves with usage any more. The band's constants (`SkyLayout`) keep the room a red giant needed.
      - **Why this and not the others** (the candidates weighed are in DECISIONS, "The sky, physically true"): a recurrent nova's quantity that builds (the hydrogen layer on the white dwarf) is invisible, so usage would have no picture, and its eruption makes the limit the big event again; a Cepheid and an eclipsing binary are clocks, not budgets (nothing in them is spent, nothing reaches a threshold at your pace); the main-sequence → red giant → white dwarf road is one-way; a planet's day maps the clock but not the spending. The dwarf-nova disk is the one real, periodic, threshold-driven system whose light is its rate and whose size is its reservoir.
    - **No flapping, no replays** (owner's feedback: the explosion and rebirth kept repeating).
      - A lifted limit only counts at its reset time or after it has held for 20s. A stale reading of the window that just reset isn't a limit at all.
      - The same limit coming back (its start time, or its reset still ahead and within 2 minutes of the last one) never drains twice. The week has the same guard, so its low state never drains twice.
      - Events found late aren't replayed. A limit that happened while the app wasn't watching fades to quiet over 1.4s, with no drain. An outburst whose reset (the 5-hour window's or the week's) passed more than 10s before the app saw it is simply a full disk: no refill, no ignition, and no event, so the list doesn't scroll.
      - Each limit and reset is still an event ([CosmosState.eventCount]): the home list brings the sky into view for it.
    - **The week** (owner, 2026-10-18, on the heat death and the Big Bang it replaced: "Switch to the physically honest one"; DECISIONS, "The owner's morning answers"; `fx/SkyPhysics.kt`, `LowState`). The far sky thins a little as the week fills (light = 1 − 0.65·w^1.5: nebulae faster than stars, the deep-sky objects with them).
      - **At the weekly limit, the disk's low state:** the donor stops feeding the disk, as a nova-like's donor does in a VY Sculptoris low state. Watched, it is one short, calm transition over 4s, then still; found later it settles in 1.4s. In order: the stream and its bright spot go out first, the hot disk drains onto the white dwarf, the cool gas thins to nothing, and the white dwarf dims last to a faint point (35% of its light). The donor stays on its orbit, keeping the window's clock. Watched, the home list scrolls back to the sky so it isn't missed.
      - **Nothing else ends.** The far sky stays as it is (no star burns out, nothing goes dark); the planets stand on their orbits, lit at 60% as round a quiet disk, their warm tint gone with the disk's gas; names, orbits and the needs-you brackets step back to 70% as round a quiet disk. No overlay, and the app keeps its colours.
      - **The wait:** the meters are the source of truth. Under the sun, in the 5-hour wait's words, `REFILLS IN 2d 4h` (muted: nothing refills until the week's reset), measured again only when its words change; the ring isn't drawn, as round a quiet disk. The account card says the week (`The week's budget is spent`, `returns in 2d 4h`), TalkBack "Weekly limit reached, resets in 2d 4h". Nothing moves, so the sky asks for the far sky's slow frames only (measured on the emulator, 2026-10-18: 6.4 frames a second, as at rest).
      - **It outranks the 5-hour window.** Both spent, the low state shows, with the week's countdown.
      - **The week's reset:** the stream comes back and refills the disk over 1.2s (the stream first, the cool gas piling up behind it, the white dwarf back to its light), then the disk ignites as at the 5-hour reset, the heating front sweeping in from the rim, its first flush at the inner edge half as bright again. A 5-hour limit that still holds takes over from there (its quiet disk fades in, as one found later does).
    - **Eclipse** (signed out, plan ended; `CosmosState.eclipse` takes the line to show). A dark disc a hair larger than the disk's rim slides over the system, slowing at contact for a diamond ring; at totality only the disk's light scattered wide beyond the moon and a thin bright edge remain, with the caller's line under it, the donor still showing on its orbit outside, and the planets show only their night side. When it ends, the disc slides off with a second diamond ring. Demo: long-press the sun.
    - **Timelines run on wall-clock time** (the drain, the low state's drain and refill, the outburst, the eclipse), so they finish under Reduce Motion or while the sky is hidden. Under Reduce Motion the sky redraws once a second and every effect is reduced to its end state. There is no full-screen overlay: nothing in the sky's cycle explodes.
    - **The companion (other providers).** A white dwarf in a row of its own at the bottom of the band (`SkyLayout`), 48dp from the right edge, there only while a provider is **in use**: a session on it works, needs you, or did something in the last day, as a planet is. The row goes when it does, so the sky is shorter without it. It is drawn without parallax, so taps land.
      - **Look:** a point, not a disc (a white dwarf is the size of the Earth): a 1.2dp core in ink over a 2.4dp glow at 18%, a 10dp radial halo at ink 18%, two faint 8dp spikes at ink 10%. No twinkle (no air in space). It doesn't age with usage.
      - **Moons:** sessions on other providers, in shells of 4, 7, 10 at 16, 24, 32dp, inclined 20° like the view. Each is lit on the side facing the star and wears a 1dp ring at 1.9× its radius in its own colour at 45%: mint working, dim idle, amber with the bracket when it needs you. They go round only while one of them works.
      - **Words:** as a planet's: `OPENROUTER` (or `2 PROVIDERS`) over what its sessions are doing (`working`, `needs you`, `4m ago`), right-aligned 40dp to its left. No balance and no trouble in words here.
    - **Codex's star** (`fx/CodexStar.kt`, `fx/SkySplit.kt`), only while both agents are in use; sessions go to it by their agent, not their provider. (Where Codex leads, it is the sun: its 5-hour window is the disk, its week the low state, its gas flowing at the even pace while a Codex session works, as the Mac sends it no pace; its sessions are planets and moons, and there's no second star.)
      - **Look:** the sun's kind of system at 0.6 its size (rim 7.8dp, the donor's orbit 11.7dp) in its own calmer palette (`DiskPalette.Codex`): blue-white inside, ink, then muted and dim greys to the rim; spent gas in ghost greys, so a spent Codex is a dark quiet ring, never a second red sun.
      - **Behaviour:** what the sun's disk does, with Codex's window: the hot part is the budget left; spent while watched, a 2.5s drain, then quiet and refilling until the reset; at the reset a 2.2s heating front; a used-up week is the low state. A lifted limit counts at its reset or after 20s, so it never flaps. Its drain and outburst follow the sky's clock, as the companion's ignite and fade do.
      - **Place:** the left of the companion's row, 34dp in, mirrored (its words to its right). Each keeps its side whether the other is there, so nothing moves when one comes or goes; with both there each one's words keep to their half (at least 86dp on a 360dp phone).
      - **Moons:** plain (not ringed), shells at 16, 24, 32dp, Kepler with the innermost in 8s, only while one works.
      - **Words:** `CODEX 61%` (the name in 9sp tracked caps; the share in 9sp mono, dim, amber from 80%, red 100% while a window is spent) over `needs you` / `working` / `4m ago`, `signed out` (amber) or `refills in 1h 12m`.
      - **Trouble** (signed out, `problem`): dims it to 55% and lines its rim in amber; what's wrong is on Codex's usage card. A tap on the star shows that card, a moon opens its session; TalkBack adds its window and session count, with the action "Show Codex's usage".
      - **Lifetime:** it ignites over 1.2s and fades over 2s; Reduce Motion holds it still.
    - **The companion's name** comes from every provider its sessions are on (`2 PROVIDERS`, a removed one still counted), and its words go when it fades out.
    - **Eclipse:** Claude Code's account eclipses the sun only where Claude Code is installed; where Codex leads, Codex signed out does, and the eclipsed sun's tap goes to Codex's card.
      - **Trouble:** it dims when every provider in use is in trouble, and gets a 1dp amber ring at 5.5dp for no credit or a refused key. What's wrong, and the way to fix it, is a card in the home list (see Components, "Provider card"), not decoration in the sky.
      - **Lifetime:** it ignites over 1.2 s and fades over 2 s on the wall clock. The sun's limits, its low state and its outbursts leave it alone. Reduce Motion holds it still. (The sun is a white dwarf too, with a disk and a donor; this one is bare, and has no window to show.)
    - **Frames barely allocate, transitions included.** The background stars draw from one baked atlas, and the deep-sky objects are bitmaps made once, both on a background thread. The accretion disk is one radial gradient in unit space per state of its hot region (its two fronts in 1/32 steps of the disk's width, cached eight at a time), the cool disk one more, the knots short line segments: no bitmaps, no background thread, no layers. Gradients, paths and strokes are cached (unit-space gradients per colour). Colours that move through a transition come in steps, so those caches are reused instead of a new gradient being built every frame: the planets' warm tint in eighths of the disk's light and of its gas, the quiet disk's refill and the low state's gas in 1/40 steps. No step shows. The planet-body cache holds 32 colours, so a sky with many projects doesn't miss on every planet.
  - **The calm sky** (owner, 2026-10-18, on seven planets for one live session: "it looks very overwhelming with a lot of planets ... Planets move, they little, labels move"). `fx/SkyPlan.kt` decides what's in it, `fx/SkyLayout.kt` where, `fx/Solar.kt` draws it; the first two are pure and unit-tested.
    - **Planets only for what matters now:** a project with a session that needs you, one at work (Claude, or agents it started), or one active in the last 24 hours. **Four at most**, by urgency (needs you, working, resting), then by how recently each was active, then by name. Projects on other providers' sessions alone are the companion's.
    - **Every other project is quiet:** a grain of the **belt**, 120 faint grains of dust round the outermost ring (just outside the last orbit) and a slightly brighter rock for each quiet project (up to 12, placed by its name), all still, broken where words sit. Its words, `+5 quiet` (Manrope 10sp, dim), sit in the middle of the top row; a tap shows the quiet projects' sessions in the list.
    - **Fixed places** round the sun, filled in order and kept: 0 upper right, 1 upper left (behind the sun, on the inner orbits), 2 lower right, 3 lower left (in front, on the outer ones), at x = sun ± 0.24, 0.30, 0.36, 0.40 of the width and 18–24dp above or below it. A planet keeps its place as long as it's in the sky; one that arrives takes the first free place; nothing moves when another comes or goes, or when urgency changes. Each orbit passes through its planet and is drawn whole as a faint 1dp ring (13%, tinted by the planet); the planets stand still on them.
    - **Motion means something.** At rest nothing in the system moves but the sun's slow breath. Only a project at work moves: its planet turns about its axis and its moons go round. One that needs you keeps its amber bracket drawing in every 2.6 s. A project coming into the sky grows in where its place is (0.7 s); one leaving fades and shrinks there, and its place closes after.
    - **Names in fixed slots** that can't overlap: an upper planet's name sits above it, a lower one's below the sun's readout, each kept to its half of the screen (the top row's middle is the belt's), centred over its planet where it fits and cut short with an ellipsis where it doesn't. A short tick joins planet and name. Under the name (Manrope 9sp, tracked caps, muted; amber when it needs you), what it's doing in words: `needs you` / `2 need you` in amber, `working` / `3 working` in mint, or `just now`, `4m ago`, `2h ago`, `3d ago` in dim. No bare numbers.
    - **The sky is as tall as what's in it:** a top row only with upper planets or the belt, a lower row only with lower planets, a row for the companion only while it's there, room under the readout for the hint's line only while it shows. The home list starts right under the band, so with less in the sky the list starts higher: the see-through window takes its new height at once and every item under it glides there together (no card lags over the seam). About 165dp below the brand row with everything in it, 110dp for the sun alone, instead of half the screen.
    - **Tap a planet (or its name)** and the list shows only that project's sessions: its planet gets a mint frame and the rest of the sky steps back to 42%. Tap `+5 quiet` for the quiet projects' sessions. Above the list a **filter chip** says what it shows (see Components); tap it, tap the planet again, or press Back for everything. (There's no camera flight any more: the list right under the sky already shows that project's sessions.)
    - It is drawn on the calm pace's terms: at rest it asks for the far sky's slow frames only (5 a second), and so does a quiet disk through the whole wait (nothing in it moves); a needs-you bracket asks for 30; a project at work (gas flowing through the disk), or something arriving, leaving or easing, for the live 30. Measured on the emulator (2026-10-18, demo, the tour's "nothing at work" scene, three 10 s windows each, `scratchpad/heat/measure.sh`): the disk's sky draws 6.4–6.8 frames a second at 6.7–8.0% of a core (process; main thread 2.9–3.0%, RenderThread 3.4–3.7%), the star's 6.3–6.5 at 4.9–5.6% (2.0–2.2%, 2.7–2.9%). The old sky's 5-hour wait drew the hole's disk at 30 a second (measured 21 fps at 31% of a core); the quiet disk asks for the slow 5.
  - **Sessions are moons** on their planet's equator (so their rings tilt with its axis), in shells of 4, 7, 10…; many sessions read as a ring. Moons behind the planet are drawn before it. Mint when working, dim when idle, amber when they need you. A new moon spirals out. They go round only while their project works.

  - **On the widgets, the Mac and the glasses:** the same system in each medium's means, carried over as each pass reaches it. **Mac menu bar:** the icon stays the Nova planet (it's the app's mark, not usage); the popover's USAGE meters are the disk's numbers.   - The sky window is as tall as the band (see "The calm sky"). It fades as the list scrolls over it and hides on History and Settings, and behind Statistics.
  - The sky survives rotation and recreation because it lives in the ViewModel.
  - A project that needs you turns amber inside a contracting bracket (every 2.6s, after StarPortrait).
  - A new session ignites: its moon spirals out, and its name scrambles in on its card.
  - Performance: see the calm pace above; the loop stops when the app isn't visible. With Reduce Motion it draws one static frame.
- **Grain** (`fx/Grain.kt`): grey noise with α ≤ 90/255, overlay blend, opacity 0.32, alive on every screen. Where the sky draws anyway (Home) it changes with the sky's frames, 24 patterns a second; elsewhere (a chat, Settings) it has a gentle lane of its own on the shared clock, 12 patterns a second (8 on a warm phone), whose frames redraw nothing, the sky and the blurred bars included (owner, 2026-10-17: it froze everywhere but Home after the heat work). A new pattern is the noise moved by a random whole number of cells, done by moving its layer, so only the frame is composited again.
  - Cells are 1.1dp: 0stems' ~1.5 CSS px at desktop distance, scaled for a phone held at half the distance.
  - Tiles are drawn with bilinear upscaling, like the browser does.
  - One 192² tile is generated once per process on a background thread, and drawn a tile larger than the screen so it can move.
  - The grain sits in its own layer (non-offscreen, so Overlay still blends), which means a tick never re-records the blurred content. It stays static on battery saver and on a hot phone.
- **Edges:** fully edge to edge, including the camera cutout, and locked to portrait.
  - Content scrolling under the status bar and the bottom bar is blurred with Haze: radius 22dp, bg tint 42%, full resolution.
  - The blur is at **full strength under the chrome itself** and fades to sharp only over the bar's last 35%. No black strips.
  - Each screen's content is a keyed source (`home`, `session:<id>`), and its bars blur only the cosmos plus their own screen. Without this, both screens would bleed into each other's bars during the cross-fade.
  - Android 10/11 have no RenderEffect, so a designed scrim with the same shape is drawn instead.

## Speaking wave
- Five bars 2.5 dp wide (2 dp on small marks), 2.5 dp apart, round caps, height 14 dp (12 dp in the status line and on a message). Mint `accent`; `accentDim` while the voice is starting. At rest a bar is its caps alone (a dot); the loudest fills the height.
- Motion: loudness is RMS of 20 ms of the PCM at the track's play head, dBFS −60…−10 onto 0..1. Rise 40 ms, fall 220 ms (a meter); the history moves one slot outward every 90 ms; outer bars ×(1 − 0.16·d). Driven by the shared ambient clock (30 fps, 20 on battery saver), read in draw only.

## Widgets
Two home screen widgets (`.../hypernova/widget/`, beside `ui/`), as the owner asked for them: a small one, the sun with who's working and who needs you, and a large one with up to four sessions and Allow/Deny right on the widget.

- **What RemoteViews allowed.** The launcher draws a widget, not the app (plain RemoteViews; Glance would only have added WorkManager), and that shaped the look:
  - **No app fonts.** Layouts inflate in a restricted context, so the system sans stands in at Manrope's weights (labels still tracked caps at 0.18em) and monospace for numbers and commands. The tokens are repeated as resources (`values/widget.xml`).
  - **No motion.** The sun and the planets are bitmaps drawn with `android.graphics` after `Solar.star()` and the activity meter's planet (`WidgetArt.kt`), with the sky's colours and sizes, so the widget's star at 72% is the sky's star at 72%. A widget is a still photograph: the corona is frozen at one pose, and a working planet is caught mid-turn, its meridians off-centre with a faint trail of where they were.
  - **No countdowns.** Nothing redraws a widget every minute, so times are clock times (`↻ 16:40`, `refills Sun`), which stay true until they pass.
  - **One layout per size.** Android 12+ picks among them as the widget is resized, without asking the app; older launchers get the one for the size they report. Text can't be measured before the launcher lays it out, so widths are estimated in code to choose what to say, a little generously. The one exception is the command beside Allow: it's laid out with the platform's own line breaker in the well's font (monospace 12sp at the phone's font scale), so whether it shows whole is measured, not guessed.
  - **Bitmaps are shared.** Every size shares one star (drawn at the largest any of them shows, in whole percents of usage) and the planets, and the last few are kept between updates.
- **Glass:** the GlassCard, denser: a warm near-black gradient at 88–92% (a wallpaper is busier than the sky), a faint mint nebula in the top-right corner (7%), a 1dp ink-8% ring, and highlight lines along the top (ink 25%) and bottom (ink 12%). On Android 12+ it takes the launcher's own corner radii and adds the film grain (a 192² tile), clipped to the card's outline so the square tile never shows in the corners.
- **The small widget: the sun** (2×2 by default, down to one cell).
  - Square: the star in a few faint seeded stars, its readout the 5-hour window as a **projection meter** (see "Usage meters" below): `72%` (mono 13, amber past 80%), the bar across the rest of the row, and one tag; short of room the tag goes, then the bar, the percentage never. Then the **counts** (see below), or `All quiet`; at a limit it leads with the limit as a phrase, `Session limit until 2:00` (`Weekly limit until Fri 10:00`; the permanent notification's words), then the counts, never "Session limit · back at 2:00 · 1 needs you". At a limit, or before the numbers come, the readout is its words instead (`refills 16:40`, `waiting for numbers`).
  - One cell: only the star, its percentage and an amber dot when someone needs you. Stretched wide: the star on the left, the words beside it.
  - The sun is the sky's accretion disk, still (`WidgetArt.star`): a tilted ellipse (seen 20° above its plane) whose hot span is the budget left (by area, as `DiskModel.hotSpan`), the spent part outside it cool and deep red, the white dwarf a point at its centre, white-gold inside and orange outward; at the 5-hour limit a dim red disk with its bright spot (`refills 16:40`); at the weekly limit the low state, the faint point alone (`refills Sun`), its stars as they are; drained of colour when the Mac can't be reached, a faint point with no Mac. No donor, no knots: a widget is a photograph. Built 2026-10-18 (LATER L44).

- **The large widget: sessions** (4×3 by default, from 3×2 to as tall as you like).
  - **Header:** a seam: the activity planet, the counts in tracked caps (`● 1 WORKING  ● 1 NEEDS YOU`), at a limit led by it (`SESSION LIMIT UNTIL 2:00  ● 1 NEEDS YOU`), a hairline, and the Mac's name where the counts leave room for all of it (live only; otherwise `Last updated 14:32` sits there).

Otherwise there's one Open pill, which opens the session where all of it shows; questions, plans and tools whose detail is only a summary (Edit, Write) get Open too.   - The bar is a ProgressBar in a 9dp row: a 1dp track, the **used** part a 3dp round-ended fill (mint, amber past 80%, red at the limit), and its secondary progress the **pace**'s faint fill (the colour at 30%) on to where the pace leads by the reset. When that's the limit first, the faint fill runs to the end and a 2dp **red notch** the row's height stands there (an image).
  - The one **tag**: the reset's clock time after a drawn reset glyph (the app's Reload glyph as a vector), dim (`↻ 16:40`, a weekday for a week more than 20 hours off); or, when the limit comes first, the time it comes in red after a small notch (`▮ 16:05`). Clock times, as everything on a widget.
  - The pace shows only on a live picture (it's only known while something works); a stale one draws plain meters. The model keeps the pace to a hundredth and the limit's time to the minute, so they redraw nothing for nothing, and no redraw is added: the sun widget redraws when its meter would look different, within the widgets' usual gaps.
  - TalkBack: one sentence per meter, the sun's on its star: "5-hour usage 62%. At this pace you reach the limit at 4:05 PM, before the reset at 4:40 PM." (or "… it lasts until the reset, at 4:40 PM.", "5-hour usage 72%, resets at 4:40 PM.").
  - What fits comes first: the approval, one session, the meters, more sessions. A tall widget with room to spare puts the sun above the sessions, as the sky sits over the list. With no sessions, or no Mac, the sun stands alone with one quiet line about why.
- **Agents aren't on the widgets yet.** A session whose main agent waits on agents it started reads as idle: it isn't in `2 working`, its planet is grey and at rest, and it sorts with the idle sessions. The app and the Mac count it as working.
- **Stale never looks fresh.** A widget is live only while the app is on screen or the background connection runs. Otherwise it says `Last updated 14:32` (a weekday or a date once it's older), stamped with when the Mac was last heard, not when it was redrawn. Offline it says `Not connected to your Mac`, and the star and the rows go dim.
  - An approval older than the Mac's 10-minute auto-deny isn't offered any more when the widget isn't live: its block and buttons go, and the session's amber row still says it needs you.
  - A reset time that has passed draws as a fresh window: a young star, no percentage until new numbers come, no "limit reached".
  - A live picture checks back every half hour while the phone is in use: if the system has killed the app in the meantime, the widget is redrawn as `Last updated …` rather than staying live-looking.
- **Updates:** the widgets follow the app's own state, also when no screen of the app is open (the background connection, or a process started just for the widget). Something you'd act on (who's working, who needs you, an approval, the connection) redraws at once, at most every 1.5s; anything else at most every 12s. There's no redraw timer and no update period: nothing changes, nothing is drawn. A picture that goes out of date by itself (a reset or an auto-deny passing) is redrawn by a non-waking alarm, and nothing is drawn while the screen is off (the newest state waits for it to come on).
- **Nothing secret on the home screen.** Titles, activity lines and commands are masked with the daemon's patterns for session titles (API keys and tokens, long random runs), plus what a command line carries: the value after a name like `API_KEY=`, `TOKEN:`, `--password` or `Bearer`, a URL's password, and curl's `-u`. A command that needed masking can only be opened, not allowed. The widget masks and shows at most the first 600 characters of a text (an ask's 400 and 200 more), cut back to a space, and in a curl or wget command reads a quoted string whole up to that length, so a long JSON body with `&` in it never ends the scan before a `-u`. TalkBack reads no further than what was masked.
- **Previews:** the widget picker shows WebP renders of the real widgets with generic sample data and no clock time.
- **Debug samples** (`widget/WidgetSamples.kt`): a debug build draws a believable picture into the widgets on the home screen (the SAMPLE broadcast: at rest, spending, or the limit before the reset), or into files (RENDER with `--es sample`), for screenshots without a Mac: the demo doesn't reach the widgets.

## Lens (G2)
Hypernova on Even Realities G2 glasses (`lens/`): the owner's "on the go app for developers so wherever they are they can still develop products, see results", for a developer walking with the Mac in the backpack and voice as the keyboard. The lens is 576×288, green, 16 levels (4-bit), so the look is translated rather than copied:

- **Text is the glasses' own,** by default since 2026-10-18 (Lens 0.1.10; the drawn look is an option, see "Type on the lens"). Every screen is a few firmware text boxes: one font, one size, no weight, so Manrope, JetBrains Mono, chips and colour don't exist there. Hierarchy comes from brightness instead (five text levels, from about half to full): what you act on is full, needs you and card headers a step down, the other lines dimmer, and the glance's header and the hints dimmest. Every line is measured against the firmware font (`src/core/font.ts`) and shortened with an ellipsis before it goes out, 8px kept free at the right. Glyphs the font lacks are replaced (✓ becomes ·, ▸ becomes ›) and emoji dropped. **One design with the phone:** the words are the phone's ("wants to run git push origin main", "Bash: ./gradlew build", "2 agents running", "stopped on an error", "session limit until 2:00, then carries on", "OpenRouter is out of credits", "paused after 10 minutes without an answer"), a shorter form only where a line has no room. Where the phone has chips, a line's parts stand a gap apart: three spaces between parts, five between a hint's gestures, a column of its own for the glance's projects (150px at most). No "·"-joined fields anywhere; the views carry their lines as parts (`Parts`, `gapJoin`), so the firmware look sets the gap and the drawn look draws the same parts as its seams, chips and glyphs.
- **The seam becomes the header line:** `hypernova/` and the time on the left, the spending on the right (`42%   resets in 2h 11m`; `62%   limit in 47m` when, at the current pace, the limit comes before the reset; `limit until 16:40`, `week limit until Fri 10:00`, `Fable limit until Tue 15:56`; `on usage credits`, `signed out`, `offline since 08:15`). A card's header is the app's label in caps and the project a gap apart (`NEEDS YOU   0stems`, `DONE   hyperscribe`, `PLAN   …`, `LISTENING`, `TELESCOPE   hypernova   emulator`), its age or its auto-deny on the right (`auto-deny in 8m`; no page counters: long text scrolls), and its gestures are the quiet line at the foot (`tap allow     double tap deny     swipe back`).
- **The sky becomes a planet column,** 32×144px at the left of every line (`src/lens/orrery.ts`), with the app's rules:
  - On the glance, the sun at the top is the 5-hour usage, drawn as the phone's accretion disk (`src/lens/orrery.ts`): a tilted ellipse 2px tall whose bright span grows from the centre with the budget left (by area, as the phone's), a 1px point at the centre, no pulse (a knot or two circling in only while a session works); at the limit a dim 1-level ellipse, the point and one bright rim pixel (the bright spot); at the weekly limit the low state, the white dwarf a faint point alone; an eclipse while usage can't be followed; a lifted limit is the outburst, the span lighting from the rim in over three frames. The words in the corner are the phone's either way.
  - Each line has a planet (11px across, lit from the upper left, meridians at ±45° at rest, one turn every 4s while Claude works). Without colour, amber becomes shape: a contracting focus bracket (every 2.6s) when it needs you, a thin tilted ring when a reply waits, two arcs while you talk to it, a slash on an error, and one ring going out over 1.4s when it finishes. The line in focus gets a short tick at the left edge, the star chart's tick.
  - On a card the sun gives way to that session's planet, in front of the header, as the seam's planet sits in front of its label.
- **No faint light.** The lens lights level 1 of 15 at about 37% perceived brightness, so nothing glows or fades below that: shapes are solid, shading lives between about 40% and full, and small shapes are quantised crisply instead of dithered into a speckled halo. Pictures are drawn as perceived brightness and mapped through the lens' curve (`src/lens/gray.ts`).
- **Motion is rationed,** since every frame is a Bluetooth write. Frames go out only while something moves, at most about three a second, never ahead of text. After 90s without news (the lens showing something new, from the Mac or a gesture; the clock turning "2m" into "3m" isn't news), one resting frame (bracket closed, sun steady, meridians at ±45°) and then only the clock's text, about once a minute. New text in a box is one small write; anything that moves a box rebuilds the page.
- **Telescope** fills the lens: one label line (`TELESCOPE   hypernova   emulator`, with `just now`, the zoomed quarter or "watching" on the right) over a 576×256 picture in four 288×128 tiles. A light picture is turned dark, contrast is stretched, the brightest parts are kept below full, and the rest is error-diffused to the 16 levels, tile by tile, so a change in one part of the picture costs one tile (a tile in which almost nothing moved keeps its pixels). The terminal is drawn for the lens by the Mac (#0F0D0A ground, ink, muted, dim and ghost greys, mint for Claude's ⏺ and the prompt, danger red for errors, JetBrains Mono) and goes to the nearest levels without dithering, so its text keeps clean edges. While there's no picture, its place is a framed box with the reason, and the gestures are the quiet line at the foot.
- **Pop-ups** are a framed two-line band over the bottom of the page. What it covers steps aside: a box under it goes, and one that reaches into it loses its hidden lines. Over Telescope's full-screen picture the band is the top line instead, so no tile is dropped. In moving mode it is a chime and one line.
- **The battery card** ("battery", or a swipe up from the glance's first line): drawn glasses and laptop icons with 5-segment bars (one segment per started fifth) in one small image, and the name and percent as text (`62%   charging`, `low`). A Mac without a battery, or one that is away, isn't listed. The glance has no battery line.

The pause after a step is 0.6 of a word at a comma, 1.4 at a full stop and 2.2 at a paragraph; steps are never closer than 0.3s. Text already on screen when the live view opens shows at once. A finished reply arrives the whole way through its scrolling text, the window following the newest line one line at a time; a swipe back stops it, and the end follows again.

The window is as tall as the text needs, up to what the card holds over its facts, and stays the same size however far it's scrolled, so a swipe or a step of words arriving is one text update. A swipe moves one line less than the window, so the last line in view becomes the first. ## The offline queue

A prompt typed while the Mac is away (Connecting, Reconnecting or Offline, or a send that got no answer) is not refused: it waits on the phone (`data/OfflineQueue.kt`, kept in the app's preferences, so it survives the app closing and the phone restarting; only the prompt's text and the paths of files already on the Mac).

- In the chat it is the user's own bubble, quiet, with a drawn laptop glyph and "Waiting for your Mac", and one line: "Sends itself when your Mac is back". Long-press: Edit, Copy, Remove. The composer's hint says "Your Mac is away. Prompts wait."
- Home's session card carries a "N waiting" chip (laptop glyph) beside the state chip.
- When the socket opens again the prompts go oldest first, one at a time, each under its own id as the send's `clientId`; the daemon runs one `clientId` once within 10 minutes (PROTOCOL.md, "Sending a prompt"), so a repeat after a lost answer never doubles. The bubble becomes the normal Sending/Sent one. A session that can't take it yet (an approval is open, the terminal is busy) is tried again after 20 s, a few times.
- A session the Mac no longer has: the prompt stays ("This session ended", "Start a new session with it" on the bubble and its menu; also a card on Home, since the session's own screen is gone) and starts a session in the same folder.
- New attachments can't upload while the Mac is away: the file's chip is marked and says so; the text stays in the box. Files uploaded before the Mac went away go with the prompt.
- New session while the Mac is away: the simplest honest behaviour. The sheet says "Your Mac is away. Start waits for it; your words stay here." and Start is disabled; the draft is kept as it always was. A new session is not queued.
- Demo: one waiting prompt on the first session shows the bubble and the chip (the demo's Mac is never away, so it never sends).
